1. Getting Started

Browser requirements: Chrome is strongly suggested if you plan to use the AMS data transfer.

2. Connecting Your Outlook Inbox

RiskRunway connects to your Outlook (or Gmail) inbox so it can automatically detect incoming quotes and broker replies. This uses OAuth — a secure, standard way of granting an app limited access to your email without giving it your password.

To connect:

  1. Click Check Email for the first time — this will trigger the setup/authorization flow
  2. You'll be redirected to a Microsoft (or Google) sign-in screen
  3. Sign in with your normal work email credentials and approve the requested permissions
  4. This is a one-time setup — once authorized, RiskRunway remembers the connection

What RiskRunway does — and doesn't — do with your inbox:

  • It reads emails to detect quotes and broker replies matching your configured brokers (see Section 5)
  • It does not move, delete, or file your emails
  • The only change it makes is marking an email read/unread so you can tell what's already been processed
  • To re-process an email, right-click it in your inbox and mark it unread again, then click Check Email

If your company uses a VPN or locked-down network:

  • Loading the RiskRunway site itself may require your IT/network admin to allow app.risk-runway.com through any web filtering
  • If your Microsoft 365 admin restricts third-party app approvals, they may need to separately approve RiskRunway's access in your Microsoft 365 admin settings (this is different from network/VPN access — see your IT admin if the OAuth screen fails or is blocked)

Filtering what gets pulled in: By default, RiskRunway looks back 24 days for emails from your configured brokers. You can adjust this from the filter button on the New Messages popup.

3. Understanding the Submission Board

Submissions move through a Kanban-style board with these stages:

StageWhat it means
New RiskA submission has been created but not yet sent to any broker/MGA
QuotingSubmission has been sent out and you're waiting on / comparing quotes
BindingA quote has been selected and you're finalizing the bind
BoundPolicy is bound; ready for export to your AMS

Each card represents one submission for one insured, for one coverage type. If a client has multiple lines of business with different effective dates (e.g. Workers' Comp effective Jan 1, Property effective Jan 15), they get separate cards — use the coverage type field/dropdown on the card to tell them apart.

4. Creating a New Risk (Submission)

You have two ways to start a submission:

Option A — Manual entry

  1. Click New Risk
  2. Enter the insured's name and select the coverage type
  3. Click Create Submission

Option B — Drag and drop a document

  1. Click New Risk
  2. Drag in an existing application (e.g. an ACORD 125) or even an existing quote — RiskRunway will parse it and auto-fill the submission card
  3. You can drop in multiple documents at once to create several submissions in one step

Note: RiskRunway does not generate the ACORD 125/126/140 forms for you — those still come from your AMS (EasyApps, Epic, etc.) as they do today. RiskRunway picks up from there: once you have the completed application on hand (from your AMS or your hard drive), drag it in to start the submission.

Once created, you can attach additional supporting documents to the card — loss runs, statements of value, supplemental forms, etc. — via the Documents dropdown on the card.

5. Configuring Your Brokers

Before you can submit to market, you need to set up the brokers/MGAs you work with.

  1. Go to Configure Brokers
  2. For each broker/MGA, enter:
    • Name
    • Email address
    • Delivery method: Email or Portal
  3. Save

6. Submitting to Market

Once your submission card has the application and supporting docs attached:

  1. Open the card and click Submit to Market
  2. Select which configured broker(s)/MGA(s) to send it to
  3. Review (and edit, if needed) the message that will be sent — this is what the broker will see, along with the attached documents
  4. Send

The email will appear to the broker as coming from your own connected Outlook address — not a generic RiskRunway address.

Once sent, the card automatically moves into the Quoting stage. (You can also skip this and move a card into Quoting manually if you're submitting outside the app.)

7. Checking Email & Auto-Importing Quotes

When a broker replies with a quote:

  1. Click Check Email
  2. RiskRunway scans your inbox for replies matching your configured brokers or matching an existing submission card
  3. Matches appear in a review pop-up — click on one to see what was found
  4. Click Process to parse the quote and attach it to the correct submission card

Notes:

  • Checking email does not remove anything from your actual inbox — it only marks matched emails as read
  • If a quote didn't get picked up, double check the sender's email matches what's configured for that broker in Section 5
  • You'll also get an SMS text alert when a new matching email comes in (see Section 12), even if you're away from your desk

8. Comparing Quotes

Once quotes come in, they appear attached to the submission card. Open the card to compare:

  • Each quote shows key parsed fields: carrier, premium, tax, fees, effective/expiration dates, limits, etc.
  • You can have multiple quotes on a card while comparing. You must narrow down to a single selected quote per coverage type before the card can move to Binding.

9. Saving Correspondence

If an email thread with a broker has important context you want documented on a submission (not just a throwaway reply):

  1. From the email view, click Save as Correspondence
  2. Choose which submission card to save it to
  3. RiskRunway cleans up the thread with AI — splitting it into individual messages and giving the whole thread a readable name/summary, so it's easier to scan later than raw email formatting

The saved correspondence appears under that card's Documents dropdown alongside your other attachments.

10. Moving to Binding

Once a single quote is selected for a coverage type, click Into Bind (or drag the card manually) to move it to the Binding stage.

If you still need to send a formal bind request to your broker/MGA (with any required subjectivities/supplemental forms), do that the same way you always have — via email — and attach the confirmation into the card once you have it.

Once the broker confirms the bind and you've received the binder/invoice (and eventually the policy + dec page), click Mark as Bound.

11. Exporting to Your AMS

RiskRunway does not directly write into any AMS — instead, it fills in the screen you're looking at, and you review and save it yourself. You are always the final check before anything is saved.

A. Web-based AMS

  1. From a Bound card, click Export to AMS
  2. Choose Web-based
  3. This uses the Chrome extension — a list of your currently open browser tabs will appear
  4. Select the tab where your AMS is open
  5. RiskRunway will fill in the fields it can find on that screen automatically
  6. Review every field before saving — carefully check premiums, limits, coverage terms, and named insured details in particular

B. Desktop AMS

  1. From a Bound card, click Export to AMS
  2. Choose Desktop
  3. This uses the AMS Com local agent installed on your computer (see the setup guide for installation and Mac permission steps)
  4. An AMS Agent popup will appear. Place this popup on the target AMS window and click 'Push Data Here'
  5. Once it's complete, review every field before saving

Reminder: Quote PDFs over 5 pages may not parse reliably (see Section 14).

12. SMS Alerts

RiskRunway can text you when a matching broker email comes in, so you don't have to be at your desk.

To set up:

  1. Go to SMS Alerts, bottom-left of main screen
  2. Enter your phone number including the country code, e.g. +12255551234
  3. Click Send Test to confirm it's working
  4. Click Save

Once set up, any email matching your configured brokers (Section 5) will trigger a text summary — even before you click Check Email yourself. You can reply to the text or open the app to process it.

13. Reporting a Bug

If something looks wrong or broken:

  1. Click the menu icon (top right, "hamburger" icon)
  2. Click Report Bug
  3. This takes a screenshot of your current screen
  4. Use the drawing tool to circle or highlight the area you're referring to
  5. Add a description of the issue at the bottom
  6. Click Send Bug Report

This is the fastest way to get an issue in front of the RiskRunway team — no need to email or call separately.
